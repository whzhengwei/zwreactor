Reactor Maintenance Guide

Maintenance · 7 min read
WEIHAI ZHENGWEI MACHINERY CO.,LTD · Chemical Reactor Specialists

A well-maintained reactor runs longer, performs better, and costs less to operate than one that is neglected until something breaks. The difference between planned maintenance and emergency repair is often measured in days of lost production — and the cost of that downtime far exceeds any maintenance budget.

This guide covers the essential maintenance tasks for chemical reactors, organized by frequency. Follow these schedules and you will catch problems before they become failures.

1. Daily Checks — What the Operator Should Monitor

Every shift, the operator should check:

  • Agitator motor current: Unusual spikes indicate bearing wear or increasing viscosity
  • Seal area: Any visible leakage, even slight weeping, should be logged
  • Temperature gauges: Compare indicated vs. actual (if possible)
  • Pressure gauges: Note any drift from calibration
  • Lubrication levels: Gear box oil level and condition

2. Weekly Checks — Deeper Inspection

Once a week:

  • Check agitator shaft alignment — misalignment causes seal wear and bearing failure
  • Inspect seal faces for wear patterns
  • Verify that all instrument connections are tight
  • Check for unusual vibration or noise from the agitator drive

3. Monthly Maintenance — Preventive Actions

Monthly tasks:

  • Lubricate bearings and gear boxes per manufacturer schedule
  • Calibrate temperature and pressure instruments
  • Inspect jacket for corrosion, especially at inlet/outlet connections
  • Check agitator blade condition — erosion, cracking, or buildup
  • Test safety relief valves (functional test, not full certification)

4. Annual Inspection — Comprehensive Review

Once per year:

  • Hydrostatic test: Per ASME or local code requirements
  • Internal inspection: Check for corrosion, erosion, weld condition
  • NDT on critical welds: PT, MT, or UT as required by service
  • Agitator shaft deflection check
  • Seal replacement: Even if no leakage is visible — seals degrade internally
  • Nameplate and documentation verification

5. Seal Replacement — The Most Important Preventive Task

Mechanical seals and magnetic coupling seals are the most failure-prone components in a reactor. Even when a seal appears to be working, internal wear patterns develop that will eventually cause leakage.

Recommended seal replacement interval: Every 12-24 months for mechanical seals, depending on service conditions. Magnetic coupling seals typically last longer but should be inspected annually for magnet degradation and eddy current heating.

Maintenance is only effective if you track it. Keep a log for each reactor:
